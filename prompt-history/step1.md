HERE ARE THE STYLE GUIDELINES FOR THIS ANIMATION WE ARE MAKING:
Animation Style Guide: "Sophisticated Minimalism"
Overall Philosophy:
The core aesthetic is a blend of clean, modern minimalism and a warm, tactile, analog feel. The style is sophisticated, informative, and calm. Every element and movement should feel deliberate and serve to clarify the concept being explained. The goal is to make complex ideas feel simple, elegant, and approachable.
I. Visual Aesthetics & Appearance
1. Color Palette:
The palette is extremely limited and must be adhered to strictly.
Primary Background: A soft, warm off-white or light cream. Never pure white (#FFFFFF). This is crucial for the warm, approachable feel.
Primary Accent Color: A muted, earthy mint green (#74b49b). This is the main color for shapes, icons, and highlighted text.
Secondary Accent Color: A darker, more desaturated shade of the same green (#066641). Used for contrast or to show states (e.g., the stick figure changing color). 
Tertiary Accent Color: a salmon color used sometimes (#ff5e4d)
Primary Text & Lines: A deep charcoal or near-black. Avoid pure black (#000000) to maintain the softer overall aesthetic.
Secondary UI Colors: When showing UI mockups, stick to a grayscale palette (light grays, dark grays) using the primary mint green as the only color accent.
Exception Rule: External brand logos (like Reddit or Google) can retain their original colors but should be presented simply within a flat container.
2. Typography:
Typography is a defining characteristic and uses a two-font system.
Display/Headline Font: A stylized serif font with high contrast between thick and thin strokes (e.g., a modern Bodoni or a stylized Didot). It has a slightly retro, elegant feel.
Usage: For main titles, key concepts ("Emotional Resonance," "Design"), and important call-outs.
Behavior: Often used in kinetic typography, where words appear, shift, and arrange themselves dynamically.
Body/UI Font: A clean, highly legible serif or sans-serif font (e.g., a simple Garamond or a clean font like Inter or Helvetica Neue).
Usage: For labels on diagrams, text within UI mockups, and any smaller explanatory text.
Behavior: This font is typically static or appears with simple fades. Its job is to be clear and unobtrusive.
3. Graphic Elements & Iconography:
Shapes: many graphics are built from fundamental geometric shapes: circles, rectangles (with slightly rounded corners), lines, and simple pyramids.
Icons: Simple, flat, single-color (usually white on a colored background or Brand Teal/black on the neutral background). They should be instantly recognizable.
UI Mockups: Almost always simplified and in dark mode. This provides a nice visual contrast to the light background and feels modern. Animate subtle actions within the UI (e.g., numbers counting up, lists scrolling).
Illustrations: When used (e.g., the brain, hand, calendar), they have a clean, slightly retro, line-art feel. They are often grayscale or monochrome. these are good for representing concepts/ideas that can be shown more explicilty visually.
II. Animation & Motion Principles
1. Pacing and Timing:
The pacing is calm and deliberate. There is ample time for the viewer to read the text and absorb the concept before the next animation begins. Avoid rushed or frantic movements. Everything should feel controlled and graceful.
2. Key Animation Techniques & Motifs:
Blur-to-Sharp Focus: This is a signature move. Key text or graphics often first appear with a soft Gaussian blur and then smoothly animate into sharp focus. This is used for emphasis and to guide the eye.
Gentle Easing: All movement must use ease-in and ease-out curves. Objects should smoothly accelerate and decelerate. No linear, robotic motion.
Sequential Reveals: Use sequential animations to explain a process. For example, in a flowchart, each node and its connecting line should appear one after the other, not all at once. This builds the idea step-by-step.
Simple Fades: The primary transition is a simple fade-in and fade-out. This keeps the focus on the content without distracting the viewer.
Scale & Pop: Objects can appear by scaling up from 0% to 100% very quickly, with a slight overshoot and settle (a "pop" effect). This should be subtle and fast.
Line Drawing: Lines (like those in flowcharts or connecting elements) should animate as if they are being drawn onto the screen.
Kinetic Typography: Reserved for the Display Font. Words can slide into place, type on, or rearrange themselves to form a title. The motion should still be smooth and controlled.
III. Composition & Layout
Framing: All animations are designed for a vertical (9:16) aspect ratio. Compositions should be strong within this frame.
Balance & Negative Space: Compositions are generally centered and balanced. Make generous use of negative space to keep the screen from feeling cluttered. This enhances the minimalist and sophisticated feel.
Visual Hierarchy: Use size, color, and motion to create a clear visual hierarchy. The most important element on the screen should be immediately obvious.
IV. What to Avoid (Don'ts)
NO complex 3D graphics.
NO breaking the established color palette.
NO overly complex or decorative illustrations. Simplicity is key.
NO jarring or sudden movements.
